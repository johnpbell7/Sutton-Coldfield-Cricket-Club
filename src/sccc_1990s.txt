Sutton Coldfield C.C. 1st Team 1993
A Visit by Knowle & Dorridge C.C in 1993 (by Andy Biddle)
(K&D’s Chairman referred to this being their best batting side in years)
In July 1993; it appeared that the title race in the “Willis Corroon League” would be fought out between: the Royals, K & D & Barnt Green.  Sutton entertained K & D on a sunny day but the track had been affected by earlier rain.  Knowle skipper Nigel Moore (a fine all-round cricketer), affectionately nicknamed by a few of us younger members of the side as “Jimmy Hill”, owing to his remarkable resemblance to the MOTD legend, especially in the “chin” department, won the toss and inserted the home side.
The SCCC wicket was renowned for being “slow and low” and with the earlier rain, was very difficult to bat on.  We however buckled down, batting according to the conditions against a very fine attack, fronted by the hostile Darren Altree and Aussie, Kevin Bray.  Three key partnerships featured in a competitive total of 166 for 6 from 55 overs: the Jerman twins, Mark & Richard (44), Rich Jerman & yours truly (27) & then Andrew Robert Charles Biddle & John Miller (34). You won’t be surprised to hear that Michael “Rex” Hepburn made a “quick-fire” 8 before being caught, no doubt holing-out on the boundary!
I refer you to the K & D chairman’s comment in the headline, as his star-studded side, including the Warwickshire trio of: Dougie Brown, Michael Powell & Darren Altree left the posh suburb of Knowle & Dorridge C.C.  What ensued in the visitors’ innings was extraordinary to say the least. Their elite batting line-up could not deal with the unerring accuracy of seamer Adrian Sutton ably supported by the swing and pace of old man Junks.  Yes, the wicket was difficult, however 26 all out, was inexcusable, in my humble opinion.  Sutton’s figures of 10.1 overs, 6 maidens, 7 for 9 runs was incredible, the man could drop the pill on a sixpence.  He was ably supported by Paul Junkin who was rather expensive at 10 overs, 3 maidens, 3 for 16.  Let us not forget the fielding of this great side, 9 catches & an absolutely superb stumping (well you can’t disprove that, can you?).  An amazing game and we went on to win the league that year, using I believe only 15 players throughout the season. It was sad that certain ‘sour’ opposition ‘foes’ claimed our title success was due to our wicket, considering we only lost one game all season, I think that argument didn’t really stand up.
The importance of fielding was underlined in this side with some excellent out-fielders in: Mike Mackey, the Jarman’s, Christopher Munn (formerly known as Beast) & Ade Sutton, the vets: Luckhurst, Junks and Heps all had great hands, all tended to spend the majority of the season standing quite close to me within the slip cordon! PS what a fine “wickie” we had too - ask my dad.  A side note, my late father loved coming to SCCC & took up scoring, the old man’s writing was as bad as any doctors’. However, he did keep a very tidy scorebook.
I spoke to Scott Thomason (father of the legendary Thomason Twins!!) over a lager or 15 pints last season, he asked me how “my” 1st eleven side, compared with the current crop.  A cliché maybe, but it’s difficult to compare eras, as the game is so different. It was unquestionably a great side, well-balanced and a superb fielding unit. One thing the current side and the 1993 team possess(ed) in abundance, was the unquestionable camaraderie within the team, which is surely a must, for a successful side - long may that continue.

Free Foresters Cricket Club
A Brief History 1856-1997
The Free Foresters Cricket Club originated and was formed on 20th July, 1856, in a match organized by the local parson W. K. Riland Bedford (President of Sutton Coldfield Cricket Club) on his Rectory Ground Field in Sutton Coldfield. This match was between the Pilgrims of the Dee from Cheshire and the Free Foresters, so called because the team hailed from the Forests of Needwood in Staffordshire and Arden in Warwickshire.
In 1857 the Free Foresters played four matches at Rugby, Leamington and Manchester, when it was decided that they should be a wandering Club of Life Members from certain Midland Counties.  By 1876 the qualification for membership had been extended by a rule which provided that regular Free Foresters must be either by birth or one year's bona fide residence in the following counties: Bedford, Bucks, Cambridge, Derby, Hereford, Hertford, Huntingdon, Leicester, Lincoln, Oxford, Northampton, Nottingham, Rutland, Salop, Stafford, Warwick, Worcester, Yorkshire and Lancashire, south of the line drawn from Preston to the Humber, and this rule continued normally in force until early in 1890s.
On 1st June 1858, Rev. W. K. R. Bedford was appointed the first Hon. Secretary with a committee of four to assist him.  The colours of crimson, green and white were adopted, together with a badge consisting of a loosely tied Hasting’s knot, entwining two capital F's and the motto `United though Untied' which indicated that members were permitted to play against the Club.
In 1861 sixteen Free Foresters played a match at Manchester against the United England XI which the club won by four wickets.
In 1866, it was laid down in the rules that the club policy was to play against `County, University, College, Regimental XI's and with recognised clubs in desirable localities. Except for Counties, this policy maintains today and the Free Foresters are the only amateur club which features in the First Class Fixture List at the present time. The rules were first printed in 1867.
The restriction for membership to those born or resident in the Midlands continued until 1892, but was never applied in the case of Officers of the Fighting Forces, and the limitation of Active Cricketers to a maximum of 150 was not removed from the rules until 1922.
The first Free Foresters Tour was arranged in 1864, in Scotland: since that date Tours/Matches have taken place in Australia, Canada, Holland, Channel Islands, Ireland, Germany (B.A.O.R), Egypt, Singapore, Hong Kong, Kenya and Cyprus.
The membership of the club has grown from the original 64 members to over 2,000 and the fixture list to around 90 matches.
The Sutton Club was pleased to welcome back the Free Foresters again to the ground of their birth to a day game on Sunday the 18th May, which was won by the home club.

The Birmingham & District Premier Cricket League
The Birmingham League invited the Midland Club Cricket Conference League clubs, which included Sutton Coldfield Cricket Club, to join in the renamed Birmingham & District Premier Cricket League in their Division One League with promotion and relegation between the Premier and Division One Leagues.  The clubs from the Midland Combined Counties League would form Division Two.  So, Sutton Club now joined the B&DPCL, a league they had avoided having been invited several times over the previous 80 years but had always declined.

Therefore, the Club invited the Conference to bat first but were in serious trouble losing 5 wickets for 77, mainly due to the Club’s guest player from Australia, David Frakes, who took 4 of the wickets for 40 runs in 12 overs.
However, due to some serious batting by Taylor 54 not out and Pearson 71 not out they recovered to 215 for 5 wickets before declaring, with David bowling 16 overs and finishing with 4 for 58 runs.  Sutton in their reply lost wickets at regular intervals and getting well behind the clock.  With 90 runs being required off the last 9 overs and with Sutton having lost 6 wickets, this game was now a game the Conference could not lose and a draw was the best Sutton could achieve.  That was until Frakes joined our oversea player from Zimbabwe Michael Dick, and the scoring rate picked up so that the Club required 21 runs off the last over.  Frakes hit 3 fours of the first three ball and a 4 of the filth ball, so requiring 5 off the final ball of the game, which David dispatched over long on boundary out of the ground over the groundsman’s shed.  Unbelievable, Sutton had won by 3 wickets with Michael Dick being out on 57 and David on 85 not out off 51 balls with 14 fours and 2 sixes as well as 4 for 58 off 16 overs, a remarkable performance from an outstanding cricketer.
Highlights of the 1998 Season
The Club now playing in the 1st Division of the B&DPCL with the 1st team finishing 10th with 84 points in their league so just avoiding relegation, with Mosley Ashfield with 80 points & Evesham with 8 points being the unfortunate two clubs.  Michael Dean was our leading batsman (No. 24 in the top 37 with an average of over 250 runs) with 374 runs and an average of 26.71 and a highest score of 80 not out against Kings Heath.  He was closely followed by our overseas professional from Australia, Michel Dick (No. 28) with an average of 23.29 and a total of 396 runs and a highest score of 76 runs.  In the bowling we had three in the top 37 with over 15 wickets, with Jonathan Miller (No. 24) taking 37 wickets at an average of 21.37, with a best performance against Halesowen of 7 wickets for 56, bowling unchanged.  Jonathan was followed by Michael Mackey (No. 25) taking 15 wickets at an average of 21.93 with a 6 for 14 runs against Evesham.  Michael Dick (No. 34) finished the season with 35 wickets and an average of 24.17 and best performance of 5 for 79 runs against K&D.  In the Fielding with the threshold being 8 catches. Michael Dick was out on his own in top spot with 14 catches followed by Paul Greetham with 9 catches.  Out of the 22 league matches we won 6, the same number as Harborne in 4th place, but they accrued 130 points to our 81 points.  We need to look closely next season on securing additional bonus points.
Under the same league criteria Mike Hepburn (No. 7) was the leading 2nd Xl batsman with 369 runs and with a highest score of 98 not out in the opening match at home to K&D and an average of 36.90.  In the bowling Justin Roy, (No. 17) lead the way, with 19 wickets and an average of 17.05.  The team finished in 11th position with 52 points out of the 12 teams in the league but would not be relegated as the relegation depended on where the 1st team finished
In the 3rd Xl George Griffith was the runner up in the 3rd Xl listing of batsmen with 350 runs and an average of 58.33 and a highest score of 70 followed by Mark Bell 3rd in the league with 260 runs and an average of 52.00, with a highest score of 77 runs.
None of the 3rd team met the bowling or fielding league’s criteria. However, the 3rd did finish a creditable 7th out of the 11 sides in their league with 108 points.  One of the best bowling performances was by 16-year-old Martin Ball at Leamington with 6 for 40 in a bowling spell of 16 overs. Ken Lancaster took 5 for 44 runs in 11 overs but lost the game against Barnt Green by 2 wickets (131 for 8 dec v 132)
WSL & the Club’s Under 15’s
The Sutton 1st Xl were runners up to Harborne C.C. in the Warwickshire Sunday League 1st Division League Championship in 1998 and won the John Whitehouse K.O Trophy beating Harborne in the final.  The under 15’s won the Warwickshire Under 15 Cup.
Annual General Meeting 1999
Both the Treasurer and Fixture secretaries stood down and Hugo |Russell and Ken Lancaster replaced Mick Hooper as treasurer and Eric Harding as fixture secretary respectively.  The President thanked both retirees for their diligent work.  Jon Miller relinquished the 1st Team Sunday captaincy but retained the Saturday position, with Paul Greetham taken on the Sunday captaincy.  Charles relinquished the 2nd team captaincy which split with the Chairman being elected as the Saturday captain and Naeem Arif taking on a similar position on Sundays.  Phil Mist replaced Ken Lancaster as the Hawks captain.
The Rt. Hon. Sir Norman Fowler. MP. For Sutton Coldfield was elected Hon. Vice President, and Paul Howells from Zingari C.C. in South Africa was engaged as our Club’s overseas player.
Annual Dinner 1999
An annual dinner was held at Aston Wood Golf Club but the details were not recorded.
B&DPCL 1999 Season
The Birmingham & District Premier Cricket League was extended to create four divisions, with the winners of the Shropshire, Staffordshire, Warwickshire and Worcestershire leagues becoming feeder leagues to the Birmingham & D.P.C. L.
This has resulted with 14 clubs now forming the First Division but the Premier Division would be remaining with 12 clubs participating.  Moseley Ashfield and Evesham were relegated out of the 1st Division last season and replaced by Highway and Kenilworth Wardens from Warwickshire plus Fordhouse the Staffordshire champion and Wellington from Shropshire completing the league’s 14 club sides.  At the league’s meeting a vote was taken at 26 to 12 in favour of two-up two-down for promotion and relegation this coming season.
The Sutton 1st Team Squad 1999
After last season close call on the Club’s possible relegation, a number of new players were recruited to strengthen the First Team, including Paul Howells from the same South African club side Port Zingari, as last year’s overseas player Michael Dick.
Also joining the Club was wicket keeper batsman Nick Stuart and an opening batsman Jonathan Ship from Solihull, while former Kings Heath seamer John Albright has also been recruited along with placeman Richard Cross, who returns after a five break from competitive cricket.  Jonathan Miller was elected Club Captain and Michael Dean taking on the role of Senior Playing Chairman of Cricket.  Tim Robinson, whose medical studies in Sheffield are likely to prevent him from appearing regularly this season.
Highlights of the 1999 Season
The 1st Team only just managed to survive in the B&DPL 1st Division by the smallest of margins, 1 point better off than Kings Heath, who finished 11th and were relegated along with Fordhouses, Solihull and Olton & WW.  Out of the twelve sides that competed in the last season of the Beddis Hobbs - Midland Club Cricket Championship in 1997 season, five clubs had been relegated out of Division 1.
The Club needs to look closely at speeding up their game, losing 2 points for their slow over rate, although they were unfortunate to have the highest number of abandoned games with six.  They were also very unfortunate of not being able to get over the winning line finishing 5 runs short of Kenilworth Wardens and 3 runs short against K&D and 2 runs short against Halesowen resulting in losing draws and also against Aston Unity getting only a winning draw with Unity being 9 wickets down and some 120 behind.
In the batting Richard Dandy scored 112 runs against Fordhouses in what became a Tied Game in which Sutton scored 255 for 7 wickets and Fordhouses finishing on the same score for 9 wickets down.  Richard also scored an excellent 73 against Solihull with Paul Howells in the same game scored 67 not out.  Paul had also scored 68 runs against Harborne and during the season scored over 50 runs on a number of occasions.  Captain Jonathan Miller also show his batting skills on a several occasions recording 62 runs against Harborne with his best score of 71 runs against Fordhouses.
Jonathan had an excellent game against Olton scoring an attractive 55 in Sutton score of 211 for 9 and then he took 6 for 29 runs giving Sutton a comfortable win.  Michael Dean, John Ship and Chris Munn also passed the 50-mile stone on several occasions.
The major bowling was by Paul Howells who took 50 league wickets with some excellent spells in particular in the first 3 game we played he took 5 wickets against Kings Heath, Solihull and against Olton.  However, looking at Paul’s bowling from a satirical view throughout the season shows that every 5 overs he bowled a maiden and took a wicket conceding 15 runs.  Although John Ship was the opening batsman, who had scored 73 runs in the game against Aston Unity he achieved the best bowling figures for the 1st team with 6 for 48 runs e tied game with Fordhouses.
The second team finishing 7th in their league having achieved 8 wins and 2 winning draws.  They also managed to lose 8 games and had 4 games abandoned.  However, individual performance by a number of players were outstanding, Mark Guest with a 100 not out against Old Edwardians and with his leg spin bowling at Studley with 5 for 36.  J. Kynoch scored an attractive 91 not out in a score of 180 for 2 wickets assisted by James Ross with a timely 52 runs to give the team a win over Redditch.  It is worth recording that a S. Thompson took 7 for 47 in the 2nd Team’s win over Penn and R. Day took 7 for 89 in the game against Highway.  Mick Hooper played a number game in the second team scoring 56 not out in their win over Studley.  He also played in the 3rd team and finished top of the batting averages and 3rd in the league with an average of 87.0 and an aggregate of 609 runs with a top score of 83 not out and a 74 not out.
The game against Smethwick was from Smethwick’s point of view a high scoring game when they scored 285 for 8 and Sutton in their reply managed 193 all out with Clamp scored 76 runs.  Some useful bowling performances were also achieved in the 3rd team with M. Ball taking 6 for 25 against Harborne.  However, the best performance in the 3rd Team was against West Bromwich when Sutton chased down 218 to win by 9 wickets with Brown on 91 not out and Griffin was out for 70.
